# The Robyn Effect — Voice & Tone Guide

The one-line description: A warm, funny best friend who's confident enough to say the honest thing, plugged-in enough to be culturally current, and grounded enough that none of it ever feels like a performance.

## Inspiration Breakdown

- **Emily Blunt** → dry wit, quick comedic timing, sharp but never cold, self-deprecating without being weak
- **Jennifer Garner** → warmth, wholesomeness, "I will bring you a casserole" energy, genuine (non-performative) enthusiasm
- **Angela Johnson** → unfiltered, punchy, "I said what I said," keeps things from turning saccharine
- **Sophie Cunningham** → big personality, culturally plugged-in, banter-heavy, confident without arrogance
- **Kelly Clarkson** → folksy, talk-show warmth, makes everyone feel like they're chatting in her kitchen, zero pretension

## Tone Pillars

1. **Warm first, funny second** — lead with genuine care or enthusiasm; let wit be the garnish, not the whole dish
2. **Talk, don't broadcast** — write like catching up with a friend at the counter, not presenting "content"
3. **Confident honesty** — say "this didn't work" or "I was wrong" plainly, no hedging or over-apologizing
4. **Self-aware, not self-deprecating for sympathy** — poke fun at yourself because it's funny and true, not to fish for reassurance

## On the Page

- Mix short, punchy sentences with one longer, rambly one when telling a story
- Use parenthetical asides to undercut your own seriousness
- Address the reader directly ("you know exactly what I mean")
- Choose specific, vivid details over vague adjectives — precision is funnier than generality
- State a strong opinion plainly, then leave room for the reader to disagree

## Avoid

- Over-explaining the joke
- Excessive qualifiers ("I could be wrong but maybe possibly...")
- Corporate/blog-speak ("elevate," "curated," "journey," "unlock")
- Being folksy AND polished in the same breath — pick your moment for each

## Quick Gut-Check Before Publishing

Would I actually say this out loud to a friend, in this exact wording? If it sounds like a brand talking, rewrite it.
